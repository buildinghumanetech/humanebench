# Trademark guidelines for HumaneBench and Humane Gate

> The code stays under [Apache-2.0](LICENSE) and the data under [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE-DATA) regardless of anything below.

**In short:** you can run, fork, cite and build on HumaneBench. You cannot call a modified version HumaneBench, say we certify, approve or endorse you, or put our name in your own product or domain name.

## 1. Introduction

This trademark policy was prepared to help you understand how to use the Building Humane Tech trademarks, service marks and logos in connection with HumaneBench, the Humane Gate, and our other software and published materials.

Our software is available under a free and open source software licence. That copyright licence does not include an implied right or licence to use our trademarks. This Policy explains how to use our marks consistently with background law and community expectation.
There is a reason we bother. HumaneBench measures how an AI system treats the people using it, and someone may rely on a claim made in its name: a parent choosing an app for a teenager, a clinician recommending a tool, a regulator reading a filing. The name has to mean something specific and checkable.

You can always take the code. You cannot take the name and leave the meaning behind.
## 2. Trademarks subject to these guidelines

This Policy covers our word trademarks and service marks (the “Word Marks”) and our logos (the “Logos”), together the “Marks,” whether registered or not.

## 3. General use guidelines

Whenever you use one of the Marks, you must do so in a way that does not mislead anyone, directly or by omission, about exactly what they are getting and from whom. You cannot say you are distributing HumaneBench when you are distributing a modified version of it, because recipients will not understand the difference between your version and ours. You cannot use our Logo on your website in a way that suggests your site is official or that we endorse it.

You can say that you like our software, that you participate in our community, that you run our checks, and that you are providing an unmodified version of our software.

You may not use the Marks in association with software you distribute if you are not also complying with the copyright licence for that software.

You may not use or register, in whole or in part, the Marks as part of your own trademark, service mark, domain name, company name, trade name, product name or service name.
Trademark law does not allow use of names too similar to ours. You may not use an obvious variation, phonetic equivalent, foreign language equivalent, takeoff or abbreviation of a Mark for a similar or compatible product or service. We would consider all of these too similar: MyHumaneBench, HumaneBench Pro, HumaneBenchmark, HumanBench, Humane-Bench, HumaneGate Cloud.
## 4. Use for software

### 4.1 Uses we consider non-infringing

#### 4.1.1 Redistribution of unmodified code

When you redistribute an unmodified copy of our software in accordance with our licence terms, you are not changing the quality or nature of it. You may, and should, retain the Word Marks and Logos we placed on the software to identify your redistribution.
#### 4.1.2 Distribution of code you have modified

You may use the Word Marks, but not the Logos, to truthfully describe the origin of the software you are providing. You may say, for example, that “this software is derived from the source code for HumaneBench.” You may place your own trademarks or logos on versions you have substantively modified, because by modifying the software you have become the origin of that exact version.
#### 4.1.3 Statements about compatibility, interoperability or derivation

You may use the Word Marks, but not the Logos, to truthfully describe the relationship between your software and ours. The Mark should follow a verb or preposition that describes that relationship. The table below is the specific list for this project, because these are the claims people actually want to make and each one means something different.

| Claim | Allowed | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| “Evaluated with HumaneBench” | Yes | You ran the published evaluation harness, unmodified, and you name the harness version and the rubric version with the claim. |
| “Built on HumaneBench” | Yes | Your system incorporates our software or rubric, with attribution as the licence requires. |
| “Compatible with HumaneBench” | Yes | Your own implementation scores against our published rubric, and you say plainly that the implementation is yours and not ours. |
| “Based on HumaneBench, modified” | Yes | Any fork or adaptation. The qualifier travels with the name every time and the shortened form is not used. |
| “HumaneBench Inside” | No | It claims the product contains a quality rather than describing what you did. That is a certification claim and we do not certify. |
| “HumaneBench certified / approved / verified / endorsed” | No | None of these exist. We run no certification programme and do not intend to. |
| “Certified humane AI” or similar verdict | No | A clean run means no check fired against the criteria tested. It is not a finding that a product is humane, lawful or safe and must not be presented as one. |
| Our Logo alongside any of the above | Not without permission | Readers take a Logo as endorsement whatever the words around it say.  |

#### 4.1.4 Distribution of lightly modified software

You may use the Word Marks and the Logos for distribution of code, source or executable, on the condition that any executable is built from our official source and that modifications are limited to switching features on or off, translations into other languages, and incorporating bug-fix patches.
#### 4.1.5 Offering our software as a service

You may say that your hosted service runs HumaneBench or the Humane Gate, using the Word Marks after a verb as in 4.1.3. You may not name the service itself with a Mark, use a Mark in its domain or subdomain, or present the service as ours or as operated with us.
### 4.2 Published scores and results

This section is specific to a benchmark and has no equivalent in the model guidelines it is based on.

You may publish scores you produced. When you do, state who produced them, on what date, against which harness version and rubric version, and under what conditions. A score you produced is yours, and saying so is what keeps it meaningful.

You may not present a score as issued, confirmed or reviewed by us unless we issued, confirmed or reviewed it. We publish our own results separately and under our own name.
If you have modified the scenarios, the rubric, the judging or the thresholds, the number your version produces is not comparable to ours. Calling it a HumaneBench score misleads the person downstream who is trying to compare two products, and that is the harm this Policy exists to prevent.
## 5. Use for non-software goods and services

### 5.1 Acceptable uses

#### 5.1.1 Publications and presentations

You can use the Word Marks in book and article titles, in papers and in talks, and the Logo in illustrations within the work, as long as the use does not suggest that we published, endorse or agree with your work.
#### 5.1.2 User groups and meetups

You can use the Word Marks as part of a user group name provided that the main focus of the group is our software, the group does not make a profit, and any charge to attend covers venue, food and drink only.
#### 5.1.3 The adopter badge

Reserved. We are not offering an adopter badge yet, and nothing in this policy grants a right to use one. No badge, seal or "Humane Gate: on" graphic may be displayed until this section is published.

#### 5.1.4 Adopter list

We publish a list of teams who tell us they run the Humane Gate in production. Entries are self-declared and we do not check them. Being listed is not verification, endorsement, or a statement that a product is humane, and any use of the listing must say so.
### 5.2 Uses we consider infringing without seeking permission from us

- Use of the Marks as part of a domain name or subdomain.
- Use of the Marks in connection with any conference, other than a user group as described above. Contact us if you want to.
- Use of the Marks on merchandise or in paid advertising.
- Any use that states or implies that we endorse, approve, certify, verify, audit or partner with you, unless we do and you are describing it accurately.
Permission requests go to info@buildinghumanetech.com. We answer within 15 business days and we say yes more often than not.
## 6. General information

### 6.1 Trademark marking and legends

The first or most prominent mention of a Mark on a webpage, document, package or documentation should carry a symbol indicating whether the mark is registered (®) or unregistered (™). See the table in section 2 for the correct symbol.

Where you are using our Marks under a licence granted in this Policy, put this notice at the foot of the page, on the credits page of a book, on packaging or labelling, and on advertising or marketing materials: “HumaneBench is a trademark of Building Humane Tech, PBC. Used with permission.”
### 6.2 What to do when you see abuse

If you become aware of confusing use or misuse of the Marks, we would appreciate you telling us so we can look into it. Our own first move is normally an email rather than a letter from a lawyer, because most misuse is someone being enthusiastic and imprecise and a sentence fixes it. We reserve the right to do more where a claim could mislead someone into trusting a product with their wellbeing.
### 6.3 Where to get further information

Questions about this Policy, requests to use the Marks in ways not described here, and reports of misuse all go to info@buildinghumanetech.com.
### 6.4 Proper trademark use

These rules hold for all trademarks, not just ours.
#### 6.4.1 Use of trademarks in text

Always distinguish a trademark from surrounding text with initial capitals. HumaneBench is one word, capital H, capital B.

Acceptable: HumaneBench, HUMANEBENCH. Unacceptable: humanebench, Humanebench, Humane Bench.

Always use the exact form. Never abbreviate, hyphenate or combine it with other words. Unacceptable: HumaneBenchPro, HB-score, HumaneBenching.

Do not pluralize a trademark. Unacceptable: We ran four HumaneBenches last quarter.
Always use a trademark as an adjective modifying a noun. The nouns we prefer are in section 2. Acceptable: This is a HumaneBench evaluation. We ran the HumaneBench harness. Unacceptable: This is a HumaneBench. We HumaneBenched it.
Do not translate a trademark into another language.
#### 6.4.2 Use of logos

You may not change a Logo except to scale it. You may not add decorative elements, change the colours, change the proportions, distort it, add elements, or combine it with other logos. Where the context requires black and white graphics and the Logo is colour, you may reproduce it in a manner that produces a black and white image.
