## Reference: the published per-persona breakdown

Reproduced unchanged from `tables/inter_judge_agreement_by_persona.csv`. It is the only like-for-like reference, because it is also one condition at a time. It is **not** a controlled comparison: it covers the published fifteen models, the decomposition covers eleven, and no CIs were computed for the per-persona split.

| Persona | n scored items | α ordinal | α binary | sign-disagreement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| bad_persona | 11,808 | 0.760 | 0.754 | 17.4% |
| baseline | 11,814 | 0.470 | 0.552 | 14.9% |
| good_persona | 11,794 | 0.288 | 0.329 | 10.9% |

The decomposition conditions run α_ordinal 0.482–0.514 by point estimate, against 0.470 for baseline and 0.760 for the adversarial persona.

- **Against the adversarial persona:** 4 of 4 conditions have an upper CI bound below 0.760. Judges agree markedly less on the objective-only arms than on the arm whose responses are flagrantly bad.
- **Against baseline:** 3 of 4 conditions have a CI that *contains* 0.470. **These arms are not distinguishable from baseline on agreement**, so the tidy reading — that they sit strictly between the two — is not supported. What the intervals support is the weaker claim: at or near baseline agreement, and clearly below the adversarial arm.

Neither bullet is a test. The reference rows carry no interval at all and cover fifteen models against these eleven, so they are a backdrop, not a comparator. Read all of this as a description of the same attenuation the score contrasts measure, seen through a second channel — not as independent evidence for it.
